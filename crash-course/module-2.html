1.2 — Email security: SPF · DKIM · DMARC
55 min · the crown jewel skill
▶
No records published = the receiver has nothing to check = spoofers ride free

Record anatomy — annotated:

# SPF v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com include:sendgrid.net -all └ mail provider └ invoice tool └ hard-fail rest # endings: -all strict · ~all softfail (safe default) · +all = allows EVERYONE, flag it # gotcha: max 10 DNS lookups — tool-bloat silently breaks SPF; audit catches it # DMARC (_dmarc.theirbiz.com) v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc@theirbiz.com; pct=100 └ none→quarantine→reject └ where the spy reports go

The staged rollout — professional judgment, taught hard:

Locks first (SPF/DKIM) · then observe · then enforce

Verification — Myles runs these himself, and at the counter when an owner says "prove it":

dig TXT theirbiz.com # SPF among TXT records dig TXT _dmarc.theirbiz.com # DMARC — nothing = unprotected dig TXT google._domainkey.theirbiz.com # DKIM (selector varies by provider) nslookup -type=TXT theirbiz.com # same on Windows

1.3 — The account hygiene package
30 min · cheapest most valuable hour they'll buy
▶
  1. Password manager — owner + staff, shared business vault. Kills reuse, solves the sticky note, survives employee turnover. Know one manager cold; don't comparison‑shop in front of clients.
  2. 2FA on the money paths, in order: email first (it resets everything else) → bank → registrar → Google Business → payments → socials. App/hardware over SMS where allowed.

1.4 — What we do NOT sell test‑gated
15 min · recited cold at graduation
▶
  • No "hack‑proof." Nothing is. We sell dramatically harder to hurt — measurably.
  • No compliance attestations (HIPAA/PCI/SOC 2). We prepare a business for the specialist; the referral builds more trust than fake expertise.
  • Nothing mid‑incident. Active compromise = isolate, preserve evidence, call Jeremiah. Field reps never improvise forensics — cleanup destroys what insurers and investigators need.
  • No software we can't explain. No mystery antivirus resold for margin.
  • Bank credentials are never stored. Documented on the inventory itself.
  • No exaggerated findings. The report says what it says.

2.3 — The review engine
30 min · link · habit · reply · monitor
▶
A machine, not a hope — installed once, maintained on the care plan
5‑star reply"Thanks [name] — the [specific thing they mentioned] means a lot to us. See you next time." (echo one detail: proves a human read it)
Negative reply — acknowledge · own · offline · never argue publicly"[Name], thanks for the honest feedback — a [wait time] like that isn't the experience we want anyone to have. I'd like to make it right: call me directly at [number] and ask for [owner]."
Fake / maliciousOne neutral line ("We have no record of this visit and would welcome the chance to verify — please contact us"), flag via the platform's removal process, document. Never flame.
The line, said to every client in these wordsNever write fake reviews, never buy reviews, never review‑gate (filtering unhappy customers away violates platform rules and risks the listing). We build the asking machine; customers supply the truth. Clients will ask for the shady version — the answer is the same every time, and the reason lands better than the ethics alone.

25 min
▶

The tone rule over everythingCalm, unhurried, evidence‑forward. The rep who needs the sale loses it. We have a pipeline; any single "no" is fine — and it shows.
The objection bank — tap to reveal the answer
1"My nephew handles our computers."
"Great — this report will make his job easier. Want me to send it to him? Happy to walk him through it." Never fight the nephew. Recruit the nephew — half the time he becomes the internal champion.
2"We're too small to be a target."
"The fraud that hits businesses your size is automated — it doesn't know how small you are. It just found your open door. This report is it finding you."
3"We've never been hacked."
"Most businesses who get hit said the same thing the week before. But honestly — you might be right, and that's why everything here is prevention priced like prevention, not disaster recovery priced like disaster."
4"How do I know this is real?"
"Every line comes from public records about your own domain. Here — verify this one yourself right now." Open the terminal. This objection is a gift.
5"Sounds expensive."
"The whole starter is less than one no‑show weekend costs you. And the wire‑fraud version of this conversation starts at five figures."
6"I don't have time."
"You need about twenty minutes total across the whole thing. We do the work; you approve it."
7"Can you just do the Google listing part?"
"Absolutely." Unbundling is fine. A $400 client who trusts you becomes a $150/mo client. Never guilt the smaller yes.
8"My website company said they handle security."
"They might handle the website's security — this is about your email and your listing, which usually aren't theirs. Easy to check: ask them what your DMARC policy is. If they answer, you're in good hands and I'll shake yours."
9"Send me something."
"This page is the something." The findings page exists so "send me something" is a close, not an exit.
10"Is this a scam?"
Respect it — their instincts work. "Fair question, and honestly the fact that you ask is a good sign. Here's who I am, here's the local businesses we've done this for, and nothing here requires a password or a payment today. Verify the findings first."

3.3 — The company wrapper
35 min · verify with TX SOS / IRS / CPA before acting
▶
  • Entity: Texas LLC — liability separation matters the day you touch a client's DNS. Filing + operating agreement (even single‑member) + EIN + separate business bank account day one. Commingling is how small operators pierce their own shield.
  • Insurance: general liability plus tech E&O — the one that answers for "the DNS change broke our email for a day." Quotes before the first Starter ships.
  • Paper: every engagement on a signed one‑pager — scope, price, deposit, timeline, the ownership promise, the not‑included fence, an attorney‑reviewed liability clause.
  • Books: real invoicing from client one · every expense logged · fixed % of every payment moved to a tax account on receipt · CPA before the first $10k.
