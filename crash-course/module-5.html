1.1 — The 48-hour onboarding SOP
40 min · seven steps, same order, every client
▶

The first 48 hours after a deposit set the tone for the next two years. Sloppy start, nervous client forever. The seven steps, in order, within 48 hours of the deposit clearing:

  1. Welcome email (templated) — what happens next, the timeline, exactly what you need from them and by when, and the scope walls stated warmly up front.
  2. Client folder created from the template (1.2) — before anything else exists anywhere else.
  3. Credentials into the vault — domain, hosting, GBP, email. Never in texts, never in a notebook.
  4. Asset request sent — photos, logo, license numbers, the story — as one checklist, not five dribbled texts.
  5. Kickoff call booked — 20 minutes, on the calendar, within the week.
  6. Baseline screenshots captured (Module 3 habit) — reviews, GBP stats, uptime.
  7. Build slot on the calendar — real hours blocked, so the timeline in the agreement is backed by a schedule, not hope.
Why 48 hoursBuyer's remorse peaks on day 2. A client who gets a professional welcome, a clear list, and a booked call before remorse arrives never feels it. Silence after a deposit is how refund requests are born.
✓
1.2 — The client file: one folder, one truth
30 min · clone-able template
▶

Twenty clients means you will forget everything about any one of them — that's not a flaw, it's the plan. The folder remembers so you don't have to:

Clients/Rosas-Bakery/ ├── 00-agreement/ signed contract · care-plan terms ├── 01-access/ what's in the vault (names only, never passwords) ├── 02-assets/ logo · photos · owner's story voice memo ├── 03-baseline/ install-day screenshots (M3) ├── 04-reports/ every monthly report ever sent └── 05-notes/ call notes · out-of-scope asks logged (upgrade fuel)

The test: could Jeremiah cover this client for a month using only the folder? If not, the folder's incomplete. That question is also the hiring test in S3 — the folder standard is what makes a first hire possible at all.

✓
1.3 — Maintenance Monday: the batch runbook
45 min · service everyone in one sitting
▶

Servicing clients one-at-a-time as things occur to you is how 10 clients eat 40 hours. Batching is how 20 clients fit in one day. Every Monday, the same sweep, in the same order, across the whole book:

SweepPer clientBook of 20
1 · Monitors — uptime alerts, anything red overnight~1 min~20 min
2 · Backups — confirm last run + offsite copy for every site~2 min~40 min
3 · Updates — snapshot, apply, click-test (batched by platform)~5 min~90 min
4 · Reviews & GBP — new reviews get replies drafted; posts queued (Standard+)~4 min~60 min
5 · Leads check — intake sheets scanned for anything unanswered (Pro)~1 min~15 min
6 · Log it — one line per client in the delivery log → feeds the monthly report~1 min~20 min

≈4 hours for a full book of twenty. The log line is the secret: report day (1st of the month) becomes assembly, not archaeology.

2.1 — Billing rhythm & the two collection scripts
40 min · never chase money angrily again
▶
  • Care plans auto-bill on the 1st. Card on file at signup, no exceptions, no invoicing-and-hoping. "It just runs, like your other utilities" — said at signup, when everyone's happy.
  • Projects: 50% deposit, 50% at launch. The site goes live when the final invoice is paid — stated in the agreement, never as a surprise.
  • Failed card / late invoice cadence: day-7 nudge, day-14 pause. Both templated, both friendly, both automatic:
Day-7 nudge"Hey Rosa — heads up, the card on file didn't go through on the 1st (happens all the time, usually a new expiration date). Here's the update link — takes 30 seconds. Everything on the plan is still running as normal."
Day-14 pause"Following up — the card still hasn't gone through, so per the plan terms I've paused the monthly services until it's sorted. The site stays up; the maintenance just waits. Soon as the card's updated everything resumes same-day, and I'll re-run whatever was missed. Here's the link again."

Notice what's absent: guilt, threats, apology. The system enforces; you stay the friendly face of it. That separation is why clients pay late once, not habitually.

2.2 — The five-number dashboard
40 min · updated monthly, read weekly
▶

A practice is run on five numbers in one sheet. Anything more is procrastination wearing a spreadsheet:

NumberWhat it isRed line
MRRSum of all active care plansFlat 3 months straight → sales problem (back to M4 funnel math)
Active clientsPaying plans right now—
ChurnPlans lost this quarter ÷ plans at quarter start>10%/qtr → delivery problem (are reports going out?)
Avg revenue / clientMRR ÷ active clientsStuck at ~$99 → you're not attaching Standard/Pro; upgrade fuel is sitting in 05-notes
Hours / client / monthDelivery-log time ÷ clients>2 hrs → automate it, reprice it, or fire the client eating it
The hours red line is the whole ballgameTwenty clients × 2 hrs is a week of every month gone to maintenance. The moment the average creeps past 2, something is wrong with a system, a price, or one specific client — and the delivery log will name which. Feelings lie about workload; logs don't.

2.3 — The annual raise letter
30 min · +10%, earned in writing
▶

Costs rise; plans that never do become resentments you subsidize. Once a year, each client gets a raise letter that leads with a year of receipts — because you've been sending proof-of-work monthly, the raise reads as obvious instead of audacious:

Subject: Year one on your plan — and a small update Hi Rosa — one year in. Here's what the plan covered: ✓ 52 backups, zero failures · 2 restore tests passed ✓ Uptime 99.98% — one 12-min blip, fixed before you saw it ✓ Reviews: 31 → 58 (avg 4.8★) ✓ 74 updates applied · 0 broke anything ✓ 22 GBP posts · profile views up 41% year over year Starting [date, 60 days out], the plan moves from $199 to $219/mo — first change since you joined, and it keeps me able to give it the same attention. Nothing you need to do; happy to talk it through anytime. Thanks for a great year. — Myles

Rules: 60 days' notice, one raise a year max, and the receipts paragraph is longer than the raise paragraph. Clients rarely leave over 10% with a year of green checkmarks above it — they leave over surprises.

3.1 — The cancel-save protocol
40 min · a cancel is a conversation, not an event
▶

Every cancel request gets a same-day phone call — not an email, not a form. The protocol:

  1. Listen first. "Totally fine — before I process it, what changed?" The real reason is usually money, a misunderstanding of what the plan does, or a slow season. Each has a different save.
  2. Downgrade before you lose. "What if we drop you to Essentials at $99 — keep the backups and uptime, pause the rest — and revisit when the season picks up?" A $99 client is retained; a cancelled one costs thirty doors (M4 math) to replace.
  3. Lose gracefully. If they go: process it same day, confirm what they keep (everything — ownership promise), leave the door open. No guilt, no friction fees. How you lose clients is your reputation on the street you still work.
  4. Win-back at 90 days: one friendly check-in — "How's the site been? If anything's slipped, the door's open." A meaningful share come back once they've spent a quarter being their own IT guy.
✓
3.2 — The referral engine + the Pro-tier QBR
40 min · warm doors on tap
▶

The offer: $100 credit to the referrer, $100 off for the friend. Stated once at onboarding, printed on a small card, and — the part everyone skips — asked for out loud at high-water moments: right after a glowing report, a five-star review of you, or a "the phone's been ringing" comment.

The ask, at the right moment"Glad the plan's earning it. Hey — who's one other owner you know who's fighting their website the way you used to? If you send them my way, you both get a hundred bucks off. Just tell them Rosa sent you."

The Pro-tier QBR — a 15-minute quarterly call that makes $299/mo feel like a bargain: the quarter's numbers in two minutes · what's coming next quarter (their slow season, a holiday push) · one recommendation with a price if it's out of scope · the referral ask if the mood's right. Fifteen minutes, four times a year, and Pro clients never ask "what am I paying for."

✓
3.3 — The growth thresholds: when to hire, when to restructure
40 min · decisions by number, not by exhaustion
▶

Growth decisions made while drowning are bad ones, so the thresholds get set now, calmly, in writing:

TriggerThe move
20 care clients or 15 hrs/wk on maintenanceFirst hire: a part-time VA runs Maintenance Monday sweeps 1–2 and 6 from the runbook — possible only because the runbook and folder standard exist
Hours/client > 2 for one specific client, 3 months runningReprice that client or gracefully part ways — one client shouldn't tax nineteen
~Deal #10 closed (M4 comp plan)The rep→LLC conversation goes on paper: Myles's entity, the split restructure, who owns which client relationships
MRR covers base personal expensesThe "quit math" is real: project income becomes growth fuel instead of rent — this is the line where it's a company
Why thresholds are written down in advanceEvery one of these is emotional in the moment — hiring feels scary, firing a client feels rude, restructuring feels awkward. Numbers agreed on a calm Tuesday make the scary moment a checkbox. That's the whole philosophy of this module in one sentence.
